Live model of the gearless motor, its drive and the brake. Calls a heavy or light car to see motoring versus regenerating.

Call memory

Each floor has a CALL relay with a seal-in: the button contact is in parallel with the relay's own contact, so one tap is remembered. It clears only when the car stands at that floor with the door fully open (LS2).

Direction logic

CALL ABOVE and CALL BELOW compare the car position with every stored call. The car keeps its direction while calls remain ahead, then reverses, so it serves 4 before turning back for 1.

Power circuit

K_UP and K_DN are two contactors wired to the same motor with two phases swapped, so closing one runs the motor forward and the other backward. They are interlocked so both can never close. K_BRK energises brake coil Y1, which releases the spring-applied brake.

Landing

When a call is within 1.8 m ahead, SLOW drops the target speed to a 0.3 m/s creep. At the floor, the sensor closes ARR, the contactor drops and the brake sets.

Doors

K_REQ (arrival, OPEN button, photo-eye or overload) energises K_DOP, which drives door motor M2 until limit switch LS2 opens its circuit. Timer T1 counts 3 s, then K_DCL closes the door until LS1. A blocked photo-eye reverses the door. K_RUN needs LS1, so the car can't leave with an open door.

Safety

E-stop or overload opens the safety chain: K_SAFE and K_RUN drop, both contactors drop and the brake clamps. Stored calls survive and the car resumes when the fault clears.

The 3D model

A traction machine turns the sheave. The car and counterweight hang on opposite sides of it, so one rises while the other falls. The controller scans at 120 Hz like a PLC, and the 3D scene and diagrams draw the same state.
